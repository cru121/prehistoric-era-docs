Projects

City projects added by the mod. Two power the new Big Game Hunt and Stockpile systems; the third, Star-Quickening, is a late-game Fire & Stone secret-society project. Each entry below includes the mod's in-game Civilopedia explanation where one exists.

Organize a Great Hunt

Cost 24 · 🔬 Hunting

Organizes a cooperative hunting expedition. Return the carcass to a city to gain Food equal to 25–75% of the project's base Production cost, depending on the animal.

The Great Hunt

Organize a Great Hunt is a repeatable city project unlocked by Hunting. It requires a City Center.

On completion the city produces a Great Hunting Party and a target animal appears on passable land 4 to 7 tiles away. The target tile is revealed to its owner and marked on the map. The Great Hunting Party is a temporary support unit that cannot be trained or purchased.

Hunting the Target

Move the Great Hunting Party onto the target tile and use the Hunt Large Game action. The first attempt succeeds 60% of the time, and each failed attempt raises the chance by 10%, up to 90%.

A failed attempt spends the party's remaining movement and the animal flees 2 to 4 tiles away. A successful attempt removes the target and leaves the party carrying the carcass.

More than one hunt may be active at a time. A party that has not yet made a kill is not tied to the target it was sent after, and may hunt any target belonging to the same civilization.

Unloading the Kill

The reward for a hunt is received only once the carcass has been unloaded in one of your cities. Carry the party to any city you own and use the Unload Carcass action in its City Center. The reward is then granted to that city during its next yield processing, and the party is removed.

The reward is 🌾 Food, from 25% to 75% of the project's production cost depending on the animal taken. The project becomes unavailable once Bronze Working is researched.

Historical Background

Large animals could not be taken by a single hunter armed with stone weapons. Drives towards cliffs and river crossings, and the sharing of a carcass among many families, are among the oldest forms of organized cooperation known from the archaeological record.

Stockpile Supplies

Cost 24

Converts 24 🔨 Production into a Supply Bundle. Unpack it in its city of origin to grant 24 🔨 Production to that city's current production, or—after Chiefdom—carry it to another city you own for a smaller, distance-reduced grant. Only 1 Stockpile Supplies project or completed bundle may exist per player at a time. Becomes unavailable at Foreign Trade.

Stockpile Supplies

Stockpile Supplies is a repeatable city project that turns 🔨 Production into a unit that can be spent later. It is unlocked by Stone Tools, which is known from the first turn, and requires only a City Center.

On completion the city receives a Supply Bundle, a unit that cannot be trained or purchased in any other way.

Spending a Supply Bundle

A Supply Bundle has two actions. Both grant 🔨 Production measured against the project's own cost:

•Unpack Supplies, in the city that produced it: 100% of that cost

•Redistribute Supplies, in another city you own: up to 75% of that cost

Redistribute Supplies requires the Chieftainship civic. Its amount falls by one twelfth of the project cost for every three tiles between the two cities beyond the first three, though it never falls to nothing.

The 🔨 Production is applied to the receiving city's current production item. A city that is producing nothing, or that is producing Stockpile Supplies itself, cannot receive a bundle, and anything beyond what the current item needs is lost.

The Supply Limit

Only one Stockpile Supplies may exist per player at a time, counting the project in a production queue and a finished Supply Bundle together. While one exists, no other city can start the project.

Cancelling the project frees the slot immediately. The project becomes unavailable once the Foreign Trade civic is adopted.

Historical Background

Foraging peoples who consume what they collect on the day they collect it are described as having an immediate-return economy. Working materials into tools and stores that pay off later is a delayed-return economy, and the shift between the two is one of the defining changes of the late Stone Age.

Star-Quickening

Cost 20 · Atomic era · requires the Fire & Stone secret society (the Star-Forge title).

A project that provides ✨ Faith each turn and GreatEngineer Great Engineer points on completion. On completion it also grants one Star-Seed, which an Emberwright may plant to create a Baetyl Crater and turn the target and adjacent tiles into Volcanic Soil. This project requires the Athanor building.
